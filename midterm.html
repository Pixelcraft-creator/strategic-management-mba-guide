Project Overview

📋 Assessment Details
Objective

Hands-on experience applying strategic management frameworks and tools to a real-world firm, building critical thinking, collaboration, and practical understanding of strategic decision-making.

Scope

Focus your analysis on one country, not a region or a global presence.

Weight

30 points

Midterm Project & Presentation (Assessment 3 of 4 in the syllabus).

Group & Firm
  • The brief says "Form 5 groups"; each group researches one firm of its choice
  • The firm must be approved by the professor to ensure diversity of industries and relevance to the course
Submission

Submit your presentation slides to your group coordinator, who compiles them and sends them to the professor the night before the mid-term class. No PDF files are accepted.

Integrity and AI: cite all sources in the presentation and include a proper list of all references. Plagiarism and use of AI are subject to university rules. AI may be used to support research, but must not replace critical thinking, analysis, or intellectual judgment; students remain fully responsible for quality, accuracy, integrity, and outcomes.
An ambiguity in the brief

"Form 5 groups" could mean five groups in the class or groups of five students. The brief does not say which. Confirm with your group coordinator before assuming a team size.

A & B — Background, Vision & Mission

🧭 Method (from Module 2)
1
Find the firm's own vision and mission
Quote them exactly and cite the source. If one of the two does not exist, say so — that is itself a finding.
2
Score the vision on the 5-out-of-5 test
Clear, futuristic, concise, unique, inspiring — a 1 or 0 for each with a written justification.
3
Tag the mission with the nine components
Mark each phrase with its component number; list the missing ones.
4
Revise and recount
Rewrite so every missing element is covered, and count the words (fewer than 100).
The mission must not contain numbers, percentages, ratios, or objectives, and should be inspiring and reconciliatory. A rewrite that adds components but pushes past 100 words fails the very test it is meant to pass.
The brief gives only 1.5 minutes for the background, but the vision/mission critique shares a 5% slice. Keep both tight; do not let them eat time from the 75% tools.

C — PESTEL & Five Forces

🌍 PESTEL (10%)
  • Pick one country and keep every factor in that country — the brief says a region is not acceptable
  • Cover all six forces; for each, choose factors that pass AQCD (numbers, change over time, specific to your products or regions, and actionable)
  • For each force, add the brief's required line: how it influences strategic decisions and long-term sustainability
  • Keep Political and Legal separate: the government's decision vs. the law that enforces it
Vague factors ("disposable incomes increased greatly") fail AQCD. Use dated, quantified, comparative figures from cited sources.
🕸️ Porter's Five Forces (25%)
  • Define the industry boundary explicitly — too broad or too narrow distorts every force
  • Cover all five forces, but identify which are most pressing in your industry rather than weighting all equally
  • For each force, use the lecture's conditions: for rivalry, the ten conditions of Table 3.5; for suppliers and consumers, the conditions that raise their power; for entrants, the barriers; for substitutes, falling prices and switching costs
  • End with AQCD opportunities and threats relevant to the firm's vision and mission — these feed the EFE matrix
Do not label the industry "attractive" or "unattractive". The lecture says to use the model to formulate strategies more efficiently, not to pin labels.
At 25% this is as heavy as the EFE and CPM. A one-slide force-by-force list will not show "depth of research and quality of analysis".

D — EFE Matrix

🧮 EFE Matrix (25%)
1
Brainstorm, then narrow
Build a long list from your PESTEL and Five Forces (the lecture suggests up to ~50 each of opportunities and threats), apply AQCD, then narrow to 10 opportunities and 10 threats. The brief asks the group to brainstorm and prioritize.
2
Weights summing to 1.0
Each factor 0.01–1.00 by relative importance for success in the industry. Opportunities and threats need not split the weight equally.
3
Ratings 1–4 for the firm
How effectively this firm's current strategies respond. Ratings are company-based, not industry-based.
4
Weighted scores, total, comment
Weight × rating; sum (range 1–4, average 2.5). The brief also asks you to highlight and comment on key factors and their impact on the firm's strategic position.
Do not list strategies as opportunities. "Build a factory" is a strategy; "demand is rising" is an opportunity.
Keep factors distinct — no repeated factor in two rows, and no duplicate of a PESTEL point reworded.
Check your arithmetic: weights must sum to exactly 1.00. Recompute every weighted score; the lecture's example total (2.58) was verified this way.
Learn the format from the lecture example

The worked cinema-complex example in Module 4 shows the layout, the AQCD-style wording of factors, and how to read a total. Use it to learn the format only — your factors, weights, and ratings must come from your own firm's research.

E — Competitive Profile Matrix

📊 CPM (25%)
  • Compare your firm with at least two major competitors
  • Tailor critical success factors to the industry — specific measurable ones (the lecture's airline example: on-time arrival, leg room, routes served), not "quality of service" or "financial condition". The lecture's template asks for 12 factors
  • Weights are industry-based and sum to 1.0
  • Ratings 1–4 (4 superior, 3 above average, 2 average, 1 poor), applied to each firm on each factor
  • Avoid giving the same rating to two or more firms on a factor — the matrix exists to differentiate
  • Substantiate every rating with evidence (the brief's wording). Customer-based measures such as NPS can support ratings
A CPM and an EFE are different tools: the EFE rates how well the firm handles external opportunities and threats; the CPM compares the firm with named competitors. Do not reuse EFE factors as CPM factors.
The lecture's Table 3.10 uses 8 factors "for simplicity" and notes that is too few. A matrix with only a handful of generic factors will look thin.

Before You Submit

✅ Pre-Submission Checklist
Firm approved by the professor; one country throughout, no regional or global analysis
First slide has name, job title, place of work, and photo for every member
Background ≤ 1.5 minutes; vision and mission quoted, critiqued with the tests, and revised
Rewritten mission has all nine components and is under 100 words (recount it)
PESTEL covers all six forces; factors pass AQCD; each force has its strategic-influence line
Five Forces: industry defined, most pressing forces identified, no "attractive/unattractive" label
EFE: 10 O + 10 T, no strategies listed as opportunities, weights sum to 1.00, every product recomputed, total interpreted against the 2.5 average
CPM: at least two competitors, industry-tailored factors, weights sum to 1.0, no tied ratings, every rating substantiated
Every source cited on the slide and a full reference list included
File is PowerPoint (not PDF); fits 15 minutes; every member has a speaking role
AI used only to support research — the analysis and judgment are the group's own
